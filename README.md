# Customer Value Analysis

A look at who our customers actually are, what they're worth, and how long
they stick around — so marketing and retention spend goes where it
actually pays off.

## What this answers

**1. Which customers matter most, right now.**
Customers are grouped into segments based on how recently, how often, and
how much they order. The top segment — just **8.6% of customers** —
generates **34% of all revenue**. That's a small, identifiable group worth
protecting with priority service or loyalty perks, not a number lost in
an average.

**2. What each customer is worth going forward, not just what they've
spent already.**
Every customer gets a projected lifetime value, not just a total-to-date.
The most valuable **513 customers** (about 1 in 7) account for **61% of
all expected future value** — a much sharper, more useful cut than simply
splitting customers into three equal-sized groups, which hides real
outliers among average spenders.

**3. When customers actually stop coming back.**
Tracking customers from their first order forward shows the drop-off is
steep and immediate: on average, over half of new customers don't return
at all after their first order. Whatever's done to keep people
engaged — a follow-up offer, a second-order discount — matters most in
the days right after that first purchase, not months later.

## Why it's useful

- **Spend retention budget where it counts** — on the customers actually
  worth keeping, identified by real value, not guesswork
- **Catch customers slipping away early**, while there's still time to
  win them back, instead of noticing after they're already gone
- **Stop treating all customers the same** — a handful are worth far more
  than the rest, and know exactly who they are
- Every number here is reproducible and sits behind a query — not a
  one-off estimate
